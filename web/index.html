Does natural selection favor perceiving the truth?

Most vision scientists assume it must: surely a creature that sees reality more accurately out-competes one that doesn't. This interactive reproduces Figure 14 of Mark, Marion & Hoffman (2010), which shows the opposite can hold. Three perceptual strategies compete — Truth (see reality exactly), CR3 (a coarse but honest 3-category view), and IF3 (a cheap interface that hides reality but tracks what matters). As perception gets costly, watch who survives.

Perception here is like a desktop interface: an icon isn't the file, but it guides useful action for far less. The question is whether evolution keeps the truthful view or the cheap useful one.

Mark, J. T., Marion, B. B., & Hoffman, D. D. (2010). Natural selection and veridical perceptions. Journal of Theoretical Biology, 266(4), 504–515. doi:10.1016/j.jtbi.2010.07.020. How to explore

  1. Read the triangle. Each point is a population mix of the three strategies. A corner = everyone runs that one strategy; an edge = one strategy is extinct; the interior = all three coexist. Numbers along each edge read off that strategy's share in steps of 25%.
  2. See the flow field. Every arrow shows which way selection pushes that mix, and the ring marks where your current starting mix will land (seed a point and it follows). Turn arrows off for a clean view, or on shimmer to feel the flow.
  3. Turn the cost dial. Drag the slider and watch the field re-aim. Cost is charged as a percentage of what a truth-seer earns (why: see The model).
  4. Try the presets. 1% → the field drains to Truth. 10% → it reverses to IF3. 4.36% → a knife-edge where Truth and IF3 coexist.
  5. Seed and play a trajectory. Click anywhere in the triangle to drop a starting mix, then Play to watch replicator dynamics carry it to the winner along an S-curve. Reset replays from the same seed.
  6. Find the gotchas. Seed a strategy at exactly 0% (on an edge) — it can never enter. And notice CR3 dies whenever IF3 is present, at every cost: partial truth is no refuge.

The takeaway: below a critical cost (~4.27%) the truthful strategy wins; above it, the interface that hides reality takes over. Truth and fitness are not the same thing.
